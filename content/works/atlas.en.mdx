## Problem

A travel brand doesn't just sell routes — it has to immerse users in the expedition before they book. Standard catalogs look generic: static photos, dull filters, no sense of being inside the story. The goal was to build a site that behaves like editorial: unfolding as you scroll, keeping a cinematic tone, and letting users explore routes without page reloads.

## Solution

A single self-contained `index.html` — a full landing page in vanilla JS. Visual language: deep graphite `#0B0E11`, amber accent `#D8A24A`, display antiqua Playfair Display with Cyrillic support. The hero uses a Ken Burns animation to set the scale; further sections reveal via IntersectionObserver. Each expedition card is filterable by region and opens a modal with a day-by-day program, price, and inclusions.

## What's inside

- **Hero** — full-screen Ken Burns background, meta line, and scroll cue
- **Why ATLAS** — club manifesto + 4 count-up counters triggered on viewport entry
- **Expeditions** — 6 routes with region filters (Arctic, Desert, Mountains, Ocean); each card opens a modal with program, price, included/extras
- **Journal** — horizontal scroll-snap strip of 5 stories
- **Guides** — 4 cards, grayscale to color on hover
- **FAQ** — accordion of 5 questions
- **CTA + footer** — club application form with validation and success state

## Architecture and stack

Zero dependencies: semantic markup, design tokens via CSS custom properties, hand-crafted typography. Ken Burns, reveal animations, and counters use IntersectionObserver plus CSS transitions. Route filtering and modals are vanilla JS with event delegation. The form validates name, phone/email, and shows a success state. Sticky header with backdrop-blur. External resources are limited to Google Fonts (Playfair Display, Onest, JetBrains Mono) and real Unsplash photos with lazy-loading.

## Result

A landing page that reads like a magazine: visual rhythm, smooth section reveals, and working route filters. Verified by a QA run: 16 assertions — filters, modals, form, accordion, counters, overflow, and a clean console — green on desktop and mobile.
